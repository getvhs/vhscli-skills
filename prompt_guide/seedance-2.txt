================================================================================
01  GENERAL PRINCIPLES
================================================================================

1.1  Basic Formula for Text Instructions
-----------------------------------------
Seedance 2.0 excels at following natural language logic. Combine these elements based on your
creative needs:

  LAYER 1 — Subject + Action (required)
    The logical foundation. Clearly define "who" is performing "what action".
    Example: "A woman in a red dress walks through a rainy street."

  LAYER 2 — Scene / Environment (optional)
    Define the overall tone by describing spatial background, lighting details, or visual styles.
    Example: "...neon-lit alley, wet cobblestones reflecting colored lights, moody atmosphere."

  LAYER 3 — Cinematography + Audio (optional)
    Advanced instructions can include camera movement or ambient sound effects to achieve an
    immersive, synchronized audiovisual output.
    Example: "...slow dolby tracking shot, cinematic lens flare, ambient rain sounds."

Full example prompt:
  "A woman in a red dress walks through a rainy neon-lit alley, wet cobblestones reflecting
   colored lights. Slow tracking shot, cinematic lens flare, ambient rain sounds."


1.2  Reference Control for Multimodal Inputs
---------------------------------------------
In addition to text, you can provide images, audio, or video to lock in specific qualities.
Seedance 2.0 supports deep referencing of all three media types.

In your prompt, clearly specify the reference object:
  - "Use the composition of Image 1"
  - "Match the motion of Video 2"
  - "Apply the style from Image 1"

The model automatically extracts core features from the reference material and merges them with
your text instructions, ensuring high fidelity and predictability while still allowing for
creative variation.

When uploading multiple files in a specific order, use identifiers in your prompt:
  Image 1, Image 2, ... Image N
  Video 1, Video 2, ... Video N


================================================================================
02  TEXT RENDERING
================================================================================
Seedance 2.0 supports generating on-screen text across T2V, I2V, R2V, and V2V modes.

Key capabilities:
  - Intelligent Adaptation: Automatically matches font style and color to the scene context.
  - Granular Control: Explicitly define Style (color/font), Dynamic Behavior (entrance animation,
    timing), and Layout (position within frame).

Best practices:
  - Use common vocabulary and widely recognized words. The model performs best with standard
    English lexicon.
  - Avoid rare or obscure words — high-complexity words may cause inconsistencies.
  - Minimize special symbols and non-standard punctuation to maintain visual clarity.


2.1  Slogans
------------
Prompt template:
  [Text Content] + [Timing] + [Positioning] + [Entrance/Appearance Style], [Visual Attributes
  (Color, Font Style)]

Visual Style notes:
  - Seedance 2.0 automatically identifies scene context to match the most appropriate font.
  - For strict brand consistency, use logo reference (see section 3.2).

Example prompt (Hand-drawn comic style, food scene with a fried chicken dish visible):
  "Hand-drawn comic style: Three people are sitting around a table enjoying the fried chicken
   shown in Image 1, with a friendly and joyful atmosphere. The frame then gradually blurs, and
   the text 'Bite [laugh emoji] Seedance' in order appears in the center of the screen."


2.2  Subtitles
--------------
Prompt template:
  "Display subtitles at the bottom-center with the text. The subtitles must be perfectly
   synchronized with the audio rhythm and pacing."

Voiceover example (reference: a mountain landscape image):
  "I2V: A cinematic time-lapse of a mountain landscape transitioning from a vast, starry night to
   a vibrant dawn. Voiceover: A deep, serene male voice says: 'In the vast silence of the cosmos,
   our world is but a fleeting moment. Yet, within it, life defiantly thrives.' > Text
   Integration: Render the narration as subtitles at the bottom-center. Subtitles must be
   perfectly synchronized with audio timing."

Dubbing/dialogue example (reference: two people at a coffee shop — a man and woman sitting
across from each other, menu board visible in background):
  "R2V: A cinematic shot of these two people in Image 1 chatting in a modern office. The woman
   speaks first with a playful tone: 'You always arrive right on time, don't you just love that
   perfect timing?' followed by the man's smiling reply: 'I have my own rhythm.' > Text
   Integration: Render the dialogue as subtitles at the bottom-center of the screen. Subtitles
   should appear sequentially as each character speaks."


2.3  Speech Bubbles
-------------------
Prompt template:
  "[Character] says, '[Dialogue].' Speech bubbles appear around the character containing the
   spoken text."

Example (reference: anime-style 3D character shown from two angles — front and side profile,
short bob haircut, black outfit):
  "Refer to the character design of the girl in Image 1 and Image 2. The scene is set in an apple
   field: the girl picks one apple, takes a bite, smiles and says 'This is the real deal!'. A
   speech bubble pops up beside the girl, with this line written inside."

Example (reference: two characters in a hospital hallway — a woman with a bag and a man in a
jacket, illustrated style):
  "The two characters from Image 1, both dressed in sportswear, are running on the school
   playground. The girl looks at the boy, smiling confidently as she says: 'We can definitely do
   it!'. Cut to a close-up of the boy. He hesitates and replies: 'Are you sure?'. Cut back to a
   medium close-up of the girl. She speaks in a light, upbeat tone: 'Yes!' Her demeanor is bright
   and resolute. Speech bubbles containing the corresponding lines appear around the speaking
   character."

03  IMAGE REFERENCE
================================================================================
Seedance 2.0 supports multi-perspective references for subjects, multi-image referencing for
scene layouts, storyboards, and more.

Upload images in the desired sequence. Use identifiers (Image 1, Image 2, ... Image N) in your
prompt for precise control.


3.1  Multi-Perspective Subject Reference
-----------------------------------------
Prompt template:
  "Refer to / Extract / Combine / Use the [Subject] from [Image N] to generate [Scene
   Description], maintaining consistent [Subject] features."

Clearly identify the reference objects. The model handles products, characters, animals, etc.

PRODUCTS — Consumer Electronics example:
  Reference materials: Three photos of the same mirrorless camera — front view (Image 1),
  angled front view (Image 2), rear view showing screen and controls (Image 3).

  Prompt: "Use the cameras featured in Image 1, Image 2 and Image 3. Replace the original
   background with a white one, and place the cameras on a white table. The shooting lens first
   focuses on the cameras in close-up, then slowly rotates 360° with the cameras as the main
   subject, clearly displaying the front, sides and back of each camera."

PRODUCTS — Home & Lifestyle example:
  Reference materials: Two photos of a stainless steel thermos — one standalone product shot
  (Image 1), one held in a hand (Image 2). Both have "Seedance" branding.

  Prompt: "In a warm-toned home setting, present the thermos shown in the reference image in a
   medium shot. Then smoothly push the camera into a close-up of the thermos. Next, a hand
   naturally enters the frame off-screen, gently grips the thermos body and picks it up. The
   camera follows the slight rotating motion of the hand to showcase the thermos."

CHARACTERS example:
  Reference materials: Three illustrations of the same woman from different angles — front (Image
  1), three-quarter profile (Image 2), and rear view (Image 3). She wears a beige fitted dress
  and has long curly hair.

  Prompt: "Refer to the image of the woman in Image 1, Image 2 and Image 3, and generate a scene
   of her eating a cake in a coffee shop."


3.2  Multi-Image Reference
---------------------------
Prompt template:
  "Refer to / Extract / Combine / Follow the [Description of referenced elements] from [Image N]
   to generate [Scene Description], while maintaining the consistency of [Referenced Elements]."

LOGO REFERENCE example:
  Reference materials: Image 1 shows a bold stylized "SEEDANCE" logo in blue and gold on a dark
  background. Image 2 shows a 3D cyberpunk female robot character in black bodysuit with glowing
  blue trim, standing in a futuristic neon city.

  Prompt: "The scene is set on an aerial corridor in a neon-drenched futuristic metropolis, where
   flying vehicles and holographic ads intertwine. Featuring the girl from Image 2, the sequence
   opens with a medium shot of her releasing a silver floating lantern embedded with a holographic
   projection. The camera then pulls back to reveal floating lanterns flooding the sky, which
   gradually converge at the center of the frame to form the logo from Image 1. The entire piece
   adopts a 3D cyberpunk sci-fi animation style."

MULTI-SUBJECT REFERENCE example:
  Reference materials: Image 1 shows a white Samoyed dog playing with a small white cat on a
  lawn. Image 2 shows the same Samoyed and cat cuddled together indoors.

  Prompt: "Using the cat and dog from the reference Image 1 and Image 2 as prototypes, the scene
   unfolds in a cozy apartment. The dog is lying on the ground eating dog food when the cat
   approaches, extending a paw to nudge the dog. The dog pauses its meal upon noticing the cat,
   and the cat snuggles up next to the dog. The entire scene features a warm colored tone."

MULTI-ELEMENT REFERENCE example:
  Reference materials: Five images — Image 1: illustration of a Black woman's face/portrait;
  Image 2: a blue waitress uniform/dress; Image 3: illustration of a young Black man's face;
  Image 4: interior of a retro American diner (Joe's Diner) with stools and counter; Image 5:
  pink cursive "Seedance Love" logo.

  Prompt: "The scene is set in the restaurant from image 4 with people coming and going. The girl
   from image 1, wearing the clothes from image 2, is organizing the items on the counter. The
   boy, a customer, from image 3 approaches her to ask for her contact information. The logo from
   image 5 remains in the bottom right corner throughout."

MULTI-PANEL STORYBOARD REFERENCE example:
  Reference materials: A single image (Image 1) containing a 4-panel manga-style storyboard
  showing a martial arts confrontation. Panel 1: two figures facing off at distance on snowy
  ground; Panel 2: close-up of a figure in black robes in fighting stance; Panel 3: close-up of
  a woman in red kimono with fierce expression; Panel 4: the woman in red standing amid cracked
  ground facing the robed figure.

  Prompt: "Refer to the storyboard in Image 1 to create an intense high-energy fight sequence.
   All storyboard frame compositions from Image 1 shall be presented in strict predefined order,
   after which the two characters engage in fierce, fast-paced combat."

MULTI-CHARACTER STORYBOARD example (using multiple separate character and scene images):
  Reference materials: Image 1: anime-style girl portrait (brown hair, casual top); Image 2:
  middle-aged man wearing a red checked apron, friendly expression; Image 3: sketch storyboard
  showing the girl sitting at a kitchen table watching from behind, the man cooking at the stove
  in the background; Image 4: man in plaid apron cooking in the kitchen (matches the storyboard
  layout).

  Prompt: "Refer to the storyboard composition in Image 3. A girl (her character design refers to
   Image 1) is waiting for her father to finish cooking, and she says: '[dialogue line]'. Then
   the camera pans right and cuts to the frame and composition shown in Image 4. The father (his
   character design refers to Image 2) replies to her: '[dialogue line]'. Next, the camera cuts
   back to a close-up shot of the daughter's slightly disappointed facial expression, and she
   says: '[dialogue line]'. Then the shot switches to a close-up of the father's face, and he
   says: '[dialogue line]'."

04  VIDEO REFERENCE
================================================================================
Seedance 2.0 supports video-based referencing.

- If your workflow requires a specific sequence, upload files in order. Use Video 1, Video 2,
  ... Video N in your prompts for precise mapping.
- Clearly define the relationship between the generated content and the reference source.


4.1  Motion Reference
---------------------
Prompt template:
  "Refer to the [Motion Description] from [Video N] to generate [Scene Description], keeping the
   motion details consistent."

Example 1 — Cinematic style (reference: a video clip with specific character movements):
  Reference: Video 1 contains motion reference footage. Image 1 shows an anime girl in a red
  hoodie. Image 2 shows an anime boy in a blue hoodie. Both are in an urban street setting.

  Prompt: "Refer to the character movements and shot language in Video 1 to create a fight scene
   with the character from Image 2 on the left and the character from Image 1 on the right.
   Include intense background music."

Example 2 — Marketing style (reference: a video of a horse running):
  Prompt: "Referencing the running shape of the horse in the video, generate a scene: a golden
   steed runs on the grassland, then freezes its magnificent running posture and turns into a
   horse-shaped gold pendant."


4.2  Camera Motion Reference
-----------------------------
Prompt template:
  "Refer to the [Camera Movement Description] from [Video N] to generate [Scene Description],
   keeping the cinematography consistent."

Example — Technology/Cinematic style:
  Reference: Video 1 contains a camera motion reference (diving/swooping perspective). Image 1
  shows a futuristic sci-fi tower surrounded by floating islands in the clouds, with "Seedance"
  logo text on the building.

  Prompt: "Referring to the camera movement in video 1, create a concept video for a science and
   technology park, with the tall building in the image as the visual center, also using a
   first-person diving perspective, to reflect the sense of technology in the park from image 1."


4.3  Visual Effects (VFX) Reference
-------------------------------------
Prompt template:
  "Refer to the [VFX Effects Description] from [Video N] to generate [Scene Description], keeping
   the special effects consistent."

Example 1 — Cinematic style (reference: a video with golden particle effects):
  Reference material: Image 1 shows a beautiful woman in a flowing blue hanfu (traditional
  Chinese dress) playing a bamboo flute, with dramatic mountain and waterfall backdrop,
  photorealistic quality.

  Prompt: "Refer to the golden particle effects in Video 1, so that when the character in Image 1
   plays the flute, the same particle effects surround their body."

Example 2 — Creative FX style (reference: a video with wing-formation effects):
  Reference material: Image 1 shows an anime-style schoolgirl in a sailor uniform viewed from
  behind, walking through a cherry blossom path with golden sunlight filtering through.

  Prompt: "Refer to the special effects shown in Video 1 to generate identical wings for the girl
   in Image 1, ensuring the wing formation trajectory follows the exact same motion path and
   sequence depicted in the video."


================================================================================
05  VIDEO EDITING
================================================================================
Seedance 2.0 supports video editing including: adding/removing/modifying elements, extending
duration (forward and backward), and track alignment.

If your project requires a specific sequence, upload files in order. Use "video 1," "video 2,"
... "video n" in your prompts for precise mapping.


5.1  Adding, Removing, or Modifying Elements
---------------------------------------------
Prompt templates:

  Adding:
    "At [Timestamp/Timing] and [Spatial Location] of [Video N], add [Description of intended
     element]."

  Removing:
    "Remove [Element to be deleted] from [Video N], keeping the rest of the video content
     unchanged."

  Modifying:
    "Replace [Description of element to be changed] in [Video N] with [Description of intended
     element]."

ADD ELEMENTS example:
  Reference: Video 1 shows a kitchen counter scene.
  Prompt: "Add snacks such as fried chicken and pizza to the countertop in Video 1."

REMOVE ELEMENTS example:
  Reference: Video 1 shows an office desk/table with various items.
  Prompt: "Remove everything that isn't office stuff from the table in Video 1, keeping the rest
   of the video content unchanged."

MODIFY ELEMENTS example:
  Reference: Video 1 shows a perfume bottle in a product video. Image 1 shows a face cream jar
  with "Seedance" branding on a reflective surface with neutral background.
  Prompt: "Replace the perfume featured in Video 1 with the face cream from Image 1, with all
   original motions and camera work preserved."


5.2  Extending Videos
---------------------
Prompt templates:
  - "Extend [Video N] forward/backward + [Description of extended content]"
  - "Generate content before/after [Video N] + [Description of extended content]"

NOTE: The model automatically extracts transition frames for seamless blending. The original
segments of the input video will NOT be re-generated, ensuring perfect continuity.

EXTEND FORWARD example:
  Reference: Video 1 shows a group of people meeting outdoors.
  Prompt: "Generate the content after Video 1: the two men who are late run towards them, the
   five people finally meet and have a friendly chat."

EXTEND BACKWARD example:
  Reference: Video 1 shows a scene with a man in a hoodie.
  Prompt: "Extend the opening segment of Video 1: Set up an over-the-shoulder shot of the man in
   a hoodie, and the man says: 'It's not that bad. You're just stressed. Everyone goes through
   this, you just need to keep going.'"


5.3  Completing Tracks (Multi-clip Bridging)
---------------------------------------------
Prompt template:
  [Video 1] + [Transition Description] + followed by [Video 2] + [Transition Description] +
  followed by [Video 3]

IMPORTANT LIMITS:
  - Maximum of 3 video clips as input.
  - Total combined duration must not exceed 15 seconds.

SMART TRIMMING: During generation, the model automatically trims the connecting segments of the
start and end clips, retaining only the necessary frames to ensure seamless and logical synthesis.

Example:
  Reference: Video 1 shows a leaf falling. Video 2 shows a gust of wind in a different scene.

  Prompt: "Video 1. The moment a leaf falls to the ground, it sets off a special effect of golden
   particles. A gust of wind blows by, leading into Video 2."
